1. What the app does

The app is a department reference and crew tool. EMS protocols and the shift schedule are bundled on the device so they work without signing in, including offline.

Optional Google sign-in unlocks the crew roster, in-app chat, retiree tools, and the in-app approval queue for admins and managers. Department directory, project labor agreements, and the NNSS F&R Benevolent Association may also be included and are protected by an on-device PIN, with optional biometric unlock. Some of those modules may still be placeholders until content is added.

You can use the protocols and schedule without creating an account. If you sign in, you use your Google account (the same one used for the invite-only Play testers group). The first time you sign in, you choose a display name, platoon, and station. New accounts stay pending until an admin or manager approves them, except the bootstrap admin account.

2. Information stored only on your device

The app stores the following locally. It is not sent to NGTM’s servers or to Firebase:

  • A PIN credential, stored only as a salted PBKDF2-SHA-256 hash (not the PIN itself), plus related lock state such as failed-attempt timing and whether you enabled biometric unlock. The PIN is created by you on first use of a locked section. You can remove it in the app (About → Reset app PIN). The Admin tab has a separate PIN.
  • Preferences, such as light/dark/auto theme, favorite protocols, recent protocols, and your selected shift-schedule highlight.
  • Cached content updates, if a signed content update is ever downloaded. These are kept in on-device storage (IndexedDB) and used only by the app. The current official build does not have an update server configured, so this cache is not used unless a later version enables it.

The app does not encrypt the bundled protocol files at rest. The PIN is an access gate for the directory, PLA, and Benevolent Association tabs on a shared or handled phone. It is not full-disk encryption of the app’s content. EMS protocols and the shift schedule can be opened without a PIN and without signing in.

Optional fingerprint or face unlock uses the Android biometric prompt. The app does not receive, store, or transmit your biometric template. That data stays in the device’s biometric system. The app only stores a flag that you chose to allow biometric unlock.

3. Information stored in Google Firebase

If you sign in with Google, the app uses Google Firebase (Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore) in the publisher’s Firebase project. Google processes this data on behalf of NGTM. The publisher does not sell this data and does not use it for advertising.

What is collected

  • Google sign-in identifiers: the email address and Google user ID of the account you use to sign in, and the name Google provides with that account if one is available.
  • Roster profile: the display name, platoon, and station you choose, plus any phone number you enter, your role (pending, member, manager, admin, or retiree), and account status (pending, active, retired, or disabled). Approval timestamps and the approving admin are also stored.
  • Chat messages and rooms: the text of messages you send, the time they were sent, which room they belong to, and the sender’s user ID. A copy of your display name is stored so other approved users in a room can see who wrote a message. Room membership and last-read state are also stored so unread badges can work.

The app does not collect patient information, incident reports, or personal health information. Chat includes a reminder not to post patient information. Do not put names, incidents, or medical details of patients in chat.

Who can see it

Access is limited to signed-in users whose accounts have been approved, and is further limited by role:

  • Pending users can see and edit their own profile. They cannot use crew chat or the roster until they are approved.
  • Approved members and managers can see the active roster (names, platoon, station, and role) and messages in rooms they are allowed to use (for example All Hands, their platoon or station rooms, and custom rooms they belong to).
  • Admins and managers can also see the approval queue and can approve, update, or disable accounts, including setting role, platoon, and station.
  • Retirees can use retiree tools (including the retiree directory, events, and Retiree Hall). They cannot read the full active-duty roster or active-duty chat rooms.
  • Display names in a chat room are shown to other people who can read that room.

Google, as the Firebase service provider, stores this data in its systems under the publisher’s project and may see it only as needed to operate those services, subject to Google’s privacy policy and the Firebase terms.

How to request deletion

To request that your Google sign-in, roster profile, and chat messages be deleted from the publisher’s Firebase project, email hulkemt@gmail.com from the same address you used to sign in, and say that you want your Nuke Town F&R account data deleted. The publisher will delete or de-identify your profile and messages that they reasonably can identify as yours. Some records (for example a message another person still needs for context, or a legal hold) may be retained only as required by law. Uninstalling the app or signing out does not by itself delete your cloud profile or messages.

You can also remove the app’s access to your Google account in your Google account permissions.

4. Other information that may leave the device

Live literature search (optional, user-initiated)

EMS protocol pages can include curated citations that are already bundled in the app and work offline. If you tap Search latest research, the app sends a topic search query to the National Library of Medicine (NCBI) PubMed E-utilities over HTTPS:

https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/

The request includes a medical topic query derived from the protocol you are viewing (not a free-text box of your own), a tool identifier (nnss-ems-protocols), and the publisher contact email (hulkemt@gmail.com) required by NCBI. NCBI also receives standard network information such as your IP address. The publisher does not receive a copy of this request and does not log it.

This happens only when you start a live search and only when the device is online. If you never use that button, no PubMed request is made. Curated citations and the protocol text itself do not require a network connection.

If you tap a PubMed, DOI, or similar link, your device may open that page in a browser. Those sites have their own privacy policies.

Content updates (not active in the current build)

The app can check a publisher-configured HTTPS address for a signed content manifest and, if a newer verified package is available, download it. The current official build has no update server configured, so it does not make this request. If a later version enables updates, the device would contact only that configured server, and the app would reject files that fail signature or integrity checks. Any such server would be covered by an updated policy and should not be a public, unauthenticated host for internal documents.

The app requests the Internet permission so that Google sign-in, Firebase, optional PubMed search, and future signed updates (if enabled) can run. Core protocols and the shift schedule work with the network turned off and without signing in.

5. What we do not collect

The publisher does not collect:

  • Your PIN or biometric data
  • Precise or approximate location
  • Contacts, call logs, photos, or files from your device (other than the in-app chat text you type)
  • Payment or financial information
  • Analytics, advertising identifiers, crash-reporting, or usage telemetry
  • Health or patient information about you or anyone you treat

There are no ads, no in-app purchases, and no third-party analytics or advertising SDKs in the app. The app does not use Google Analytics. Firebase is used only for Authentication and Cloud Firestore as described above.

The app displays department EMS protocols and related medical reference material. It does not collect personal health information. It is a reference to official protocols, not a tool that records patient care.

7. Backups and uninstalling

The Android app allows standard device backups. If you use Android backup, local app data — including preferences and the PIN hash — may be included in a backup stored in your Google account or on the device. The PIN itself is not stored. Cloud roster and chat data in Firebase is separate from a device backup and is not removed by uninstalling.

To remove local app data, use About → Reset app PIN, clear the app’s storage in Android settings, or uninstall the app. To delete cloud data, email hulkemt@gmail.com as described above.

8. Children

The app is intended for adult NNSS Fire & Rescue crew members and is not directed at children under 13 (or under 16 where that is the applicable age). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

9. Data security and retention

Google sign-in, roster, and chat traffic travel over HTTPS to Firebase. Live PubMed requests, when you make them, also travel over HTTPS. Information the app keeps only on the device stays on the device until you clear it or uninstall the app.

Roster profiles and chat messages are kept in Firebase until you request deletion, an admin disables or removes the account as part of managing the crew, or this service is shut down. This policy does not claim that any method of transmission or storage is perfectly secure.

10. Changes

If the app’s data practices change (for example, if a content-update server is turned on, or if additional Firebase services are added), this policy will be updated and the effective date will change. The current policy will be posted at the public URL provided in the Google Play listing.
